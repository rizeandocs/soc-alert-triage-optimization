I'm optimizing alert triage for CrowdStrike's Falcon Complete managed detection and
response (SOC) service. I have two decision variables:

1. alert_threshold: 10 to 100 in steps of 10 (10 levels). Alerts scoring below the
   threshold are not sent to an analyst.
2. analysts_per_shift: 3 to 12 (10 levels), with 3 shifts per day for 24/7 coverage.

Generate a CSV with all 100 combinations (10 x 10) showing how detection coverage and
labor cost respond to these variables.

Columns: alert_threshold, analysts_per_shift, alerts_per_day, detection_rate,
false_positive_rate, avg_triage_minutes, avg_critical_response_minutes, daily_labor_cost

Patterns to reflect:
- Lower thresholds create many more alerts, mostly false positives.
- Higher thresholds miss more real attacks, so detection_rate falls.
- Each analyst triages about 12 alerts per hour (5 minutes per alert). When alerts exceed
  capacity, detection drops and response time grows.
- daily_labor_cost depends only on staffing.
- Some combinations should be clearly worse than others (more cost and less detection).

- $73 per hour: midpoint of the $125,000-$180,000 salary in the CrowdStrike "Sr. Analyst,
  Falcon Complete (Remote)" posting (Monster, Oct 27, 2026) divided by 2,080 hours.
